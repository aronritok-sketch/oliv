Yoga FAQ

Answers to the most common questions about classes, private yoga, beginners, payment and what to bring.

✺
Am I flexible enough for yoga?

You don't need to be flexible to start yoga — flexibility is something the practice builds. My classes are for people who, between work, family, grandkids, gardening and everything else, are perfectly happy with “that felt good,” “I've landed,” “I'm going to sleep so well” or “my back doesn't hurt anymore.”

We won't be practicing handstands. Classes are varied and there's always a little challenge in them, but nothing is compulsory.

I'm a complete beginner. Which class should I take?

Beginners are warmly welcome, especially if movement is already part of your life in some way. Slow Flow is the gentlest place to start; Hatha Flow is stronger and more dynamic. If you'd like to learn the foundations first, one or two private sessions are a great way in.

How hard are the group classes?

Everyone finds something different hard. For some it's the strength work, for others the stretching or the balance — and for some, switching off their thoughts for an hour.

Hatha Flow is the stronger, more dynamic class. Slow Flow puts more emphasis on observation, at a slower pace. Both include a flow experience, which for me is the most beautiful part of yoga. Sometimes I hold strengthening poses a little longer so everyone's attention can arrive.

What is hatha yoga?

“Ha” means sun and “tha” means moon. Hatha yoga balances the active and the passive energies in us: effort and ease, strength and surrender. Think of all the opposite poles that balance each other — hot and cold, light and shadow, receiving and letting go. One doesn't exist without the other.

When that balance tips, we feel it first as discomfort or restlessness. Hatha yoga uses physical practice to find and keep the balance. The poses (asanas) are a doorway to meditation — a more dynamic doorway — and only one of the eight limbs of yoga.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes you can move in, a bottle of water, and your own mat if you have a favorite. If you need to borrow one, mention it when you book. We practice barefoot. For beach sessions, bring a large towel or a mat, and sun protection.

Where do private yoga sessions take place?

Wherever suits you: your home or lanai, your condo or HOA clubhouse, your office, the beach — or online. I teach private sessions across Fort Myers and nearby areas including Cape Coral, Fort Myers Beach, Sanibel, Estero and Bonita Springs.

Do you offer beach yoga in Fort Myers?

Yes. Sunrise and sunset sessions on the sand are available as private sessions for individuals, couples and groups, and from time to time as group events. In summer, early mornings are the most comfortable; if a storm rolls in, we reschedule.

How can I pay?

You can pay by card when you book online, or in cash at class. Private sessions and passes can also be paid in advance.

Can I give yoga as a gift?

Yes — there are gift cards for a single class or a 5-class pass. After buying, send me the recipient's name, their email address for booking, and the class they'd enjoy. I'll make out the gift card in their name so you can forward or print it.

Do you teach yoga for companies and sports teams?

Yes: team-building sessions, weekly lunchtime classes, chair and desk yoga, and strength, mobility or recovery-focused yoga for athletes and sports teams. Send your team size and location for a proposal.

I have an injury or a health condition, or I'm pregnant. Can I join?

Please check with your doctor first, and tell me before class. In a group class I'll offer options; for specific needs, a private session is often the best way to start. Yoga complements medical care — it doesn't replace it.

What can a regular yoga practice help with?

Many people come to the mat for back and lower-back tension from sitting, a stiff “phone neck” and slumped posture, restless sleep, constant tiredness, a foggy mind, weak back and core muscles, or stress. Regular hatha practice builds deep strength, supports better posture and teaches you to use your breath more consciously — and many students find they sleep and focus better. If you have a medical condition, talk to your doctor about what's right for you.
