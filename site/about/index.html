About Olivia

Hatha flow teacher, trained in Budapest, teaching in Fort Myers — and a firm believer that nobody on the next mat is watching you.

✺

Yoga found me as exercise. It stayed because it taught me to be here, now.

I came to yoga the way most people do: looking for a good way to move. What I found was a tool for getting to know myself — one that slowly brought up a kind of confidence and self-kindness I hadn't known I was missing. That is what I want to pass on in every class.

If you look at yoga only as a workout, the extra spice is that it's never just about strength. Even the toughest class holds observation, silence and permission. And strength without permission always runs out.

Training and teaching

I earned my hatha yoga teacher certification, recognized by Yoga Alliance International, in 2023 at Samadhi Yoga Studio in Budapest, Hungary, in Ádám Diószegi's teacher training.

In Budapest I taught weekly group classes in three districts — at the Buda Perinatal Center, the Prána Yoga Center and Korall Sportsziget — alongside online classes, outdoor yoga, live-music flows with handpan and tongue drum, sound yoga with guided meditation, and hatha flow and meditation sessions at the MANAS Festival. I also teach office yoga and team-building classes for companies, and mobility sessions for athletes.

Now I'm bringing the same practice to Fort Myers and Southwest Florida — to group classes, to lanais and clubhouses, and to the beach at sunrise.

How I teach

Dynamic, yet gentle. I build every class so a beginner can follow and someone who has practiced for years still enjoys it. I love the flow state — for me it's the most beautiful part of yoga — and sometimes I hold strengthening poses a little longer so everyone's attention can arrive.

You may meet a challenge in my classes, but nothing is compulsory. Nobody will look at you because you're not flexible, or because you're not yet where the person on the next mat is — or the teacher, or whoever you saw in a beautiful photo.

Awareness: I notice, I see, I observe, I allow — then I respond. Control: I want to steer it, grip it, regulate it, push it down. That is the most important difference yoga has taught me so far.

I'm proud that so many of my students come back week after week — people who resonate with this way of practicing and who are open to yoga as a whole, not just as a set of poses.

What yoga changed for me

From my own experience, and from what students have shared with me, regular practice can help loosen patterns like these:

  • a constant need to meet everyone's expectations, including your own
  • overthinking, and a mind that won't switch off at night
  • anxiety that plays the worst-case scenario on repeat
  • “stress addiction” — feeling useless unless you're busy
  • feeling empty or restless in your own company
  • losing touch with the world, burnout, the hamster wheel
  • fear of feeling your own emotions, or of trying something new
  • low self-confidence

Yoga isn't therapy and I'm not a doctor. But I've watched it give people room to breathe around all of these — myself included.
