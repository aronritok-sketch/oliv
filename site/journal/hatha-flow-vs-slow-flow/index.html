Hatha Flow vs. Slow Flow: Which Yoga Class Is Right for You?

Both classes include breath, strength, stretching, balance and relaxation. Here is how they differ — and how to choose between them.

September 16, 20261 min readPractice
✺

“Which class should I come to?” is the question I hear most often from new students in Fort Myers. There are countless styles of yoga, and the names don't always help. So here's a plain-language guide to the two group classes I teach.

What they have in common

Both are hatha yoga. Both include breathing exercises, strengthening, stretching, balance, a flow experience and a proper relaxation at the end. In both, you'll find options to make things easier or more challenging — and in both, nothing is compulsory.

Hatha Flow: the dynamic one

Hatha Flow represents the stronger, more dynamic side of the practice. We link movement and breath into flowing sequences, build heat, and sometimes hold strengthening poses a little longer so attention has time to settle. You'll work the deep core and back muscles that support your posture, and leave feeling both energized and quiet.

Choose Hatha Flow if movement is already part of your life — gym, running, tennis, pickleball, paddleboarding, long walks — and you'd like a class that challenges you while giving back mobility.

Slow Flow: the observant one

Slow Flow keeps the flow but slows everything down. The emphasis moves from effort to observation: noticing how a shape feels, where you're gripping, what happens when you let go a little. There's more floor work, more stretching and a longer rest.

Choose Slow Flow if you're new to yoga, returning after a break, sitting a lot, or finishing your days tired and wired at the same time. It's also a perfect counterweight to hard training.

Still not sure?

Ask yourself one question: do you need more energy, or more calm? If energy, try Hatha Flow. If calm, try Slow Flow. And if you'd rather learn the basics with full attention first, one or two private sessions make joining a group much easier.

You'll find both on the weekly schedule. Come as you are — nobody on the next mat is watching.
